Understanding shin pain

Shin pain usually follows a change in how much or how you train. Your running style, footwear, training surfaces and muscle strength can all contribute, so we look at the whole picture.

Common problems

What we can help with

Some of the problems we assess and treat in this area. Your physiotherapist will explain what applies to you.

  • Shin splints

    Pain along the front or inside of the shin that eases as you warm up but is worse the next morning.

  • Stress fracture

    Sharp, localised pain that gets worse with exercise and needs rest to heal.

  • Compartment syndrome

    Pain and tightness in the lower leg muscles that builds during exercise and eases when you stop.

How physiotherapy can help.

Every plan starts with a thorough assessment, so treatment is matched to your problem and your goals.

  • Running analysis with treadmill video
  • Correcting foot posture, alignment and muscle length
  • Footwear and training surface advice
  • A safe, graduated return to exercise

Common contributors

Sudden increases in training, hard or uneven surfaces, worn or unsuitable shoes, and tight or weak lower leg muscles.

See a doctor urgently if you have

  • Severe lower leg pain with numbness, pins and needles or weakness: seek medical care urgently
